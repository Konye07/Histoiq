1.Overview

Historiq is a daily history-trivia app with six quiz modes, a friends list, and per-game stats. What data exists about you depends entirely on how you use the app:

Signed in (mock Google/Apple sign-in): your account, gameplay, and friends data are stored in a hosted database so your stats and friend leaderboards work the same across every device you sign into.

Continue without login (guest): nothing about you is sent anywhere. Scores, stats, and preferences stay only in the app's local storage on your device.

Everything below states plainly which of these two modes each kind of data applies to.

2.Account information

If you choose "Continue with Google" or "Continue with Apple," the app creates an account with:

  • display name — shown on your profile and to friends on leaderboards
  • email — currently a mock value, not a real address read from Google or Apple, since real OAuth isn't connected yet (see the note below)
  • sign-in provider — which button you tapped ("google" or "apple"), display-only
  • friend code — a random 9-digit code other players can use to add you as a friend
  • a unique, anonymous account identifier, so the app's database can tell your data apart from every other player's without needing to know who you really are

About "mock" sign-in: the app doesn't yet have real Google/Apple OAuth credentials configured. Tapping either button creates a real, unique account on the app's backend, but the identity behind it — your name, email — is a placeholder, not anything actually read from your Google or Apple account. No real Google or Apple account data is read, requested, or transmitted. This will be updated if real sign-in is added later.

3.Gameplay data

For a signed-in account, the app stores:

  • daily quiz progress for each of the six games (which questions you've seen, your answers so far) — so you can close the app mid-quiz and resume later
  • your score for each game, for each day you've played it
  • aggregate stats per game: total games played, average score, and perfect-run count

This is what powers your Profile screen's stats and lets your accepted friends see your daily scores on the Friends leaderboard.

4.Friends

If you send or accept a friend request, the app stores that connection (which two accounts are connected, and whether the request is pending or accepted) so your Friends leaderboard can show their scores and theirs can show yours. Only accepted friends can see each other's daily scores — no one else can.

5.Security audit log

Every sign-in is logged with its IP address, device/user-agent info, an event label (e.g. "sign_in"), and a timestamp. This log exists solely to detect abuse (e.g. one device mass-creating accounts) and is never readable by the app itself or any client — it's written by a server-side function with no read access granted to anyone, reviewed only through direct administrative access to the database when investigating a security concern.

6.On-device-only settings

Your language choice, whether haptic vibration is on, and whether the daily reminder notification is on are stored only in the app's local settings on your device. None of these ever leave your device, whether you're signed in or playing as a guest.

7.Guest mode

Choosing "Continue without login" means the app never creates an account and never contacts the hosted database with anything identifying you. Your scores, stats, and progress still work — the daily play limit still applies — but all of it lives only in local storage on your device. It isn't backed up, and switching devices starts over. There's no friend code and no friends list in guest mode, since there's no account for anyone to connect to.

8.Sharing & third parties

None of the data above is sold, rented, or shared with any third party, advertiser, or analytics service. The app has no advertising SDK and no analytics/tracking SDK integrated — the "Watch Ad" option on hints is a placeholder that doesn't actually show an ad yet, and collects nothing when used.

The app's "Share Results" feature is the one exception worth naming explicitly: tapping it hands an image and a line of text to whatever app you pick from your device's own share sheet (Messages, a social app, etc.) — that's a one-time action you trigger yourself, not ongoing data collection, and the app has no visibility into what happens after you choose where to share it.

9.Where data is stored

Signed-in accounts, gameplay data, friends, and the security audit log are stored in a hosted Postgres database (via Supabase). Access is restricted by row-level security rules scoped to your own account and, for scores specifically, your accepted friends — nobody else's client can read your data directly. Guest-mode data never reaches this database at all; it stays in the app's local storage only.

10.Your choices & deletion

  • Change your display name anytime from Settings.
  • Turn off haptics or the daily reminder anytime from Settings — these take effect immediately and stay off until you turn them back on.
  • Delete your account from Settings → Account → Delete Account. This permanently removes your profile, friends, stats, progress, and score history from the hosted database. This cannot be undone, and none of it can be recovered afterward.
  • Guests have nothing server-side to delete; uninstalling the app removes the local data guest mode stored on your device.

11.Children's privacy

Historiq is a general-audience trivia app and isn't directed at children under 13. The app doesn't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the app, contact us using the details below and it will be removed.

12.Changes to this policy

If what the app collects or how it's used changes, this page will be updated and the "Effective" date at the top will change accordingly. Meaningful changes will also be reflected in the in-app description of this policy in Settings.
